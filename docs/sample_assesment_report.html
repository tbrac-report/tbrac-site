Key Strengths
Strong data security posture: CCPA/CPRA compliant, all U.S. user data stored exclusively on U.S.-based servers, NIST CSF framework implemented.
Clean reputational profile: no active boycott campaigns, positive or neutral U.S. media coverage, established CSR programs aligned with U.S. environmental and labor norms.
Robust IP portfolio: USPTO-registered patents covering core products, active trademark defense program, clean litigation history.
Transparent corporate governance: majority-independent board, HFCAA-compliant with full PCAOB audit access, SOX 302/404 certifications in place.
Fully private ownership structure with no state-owned enterprise equity or embedded CCP governance units — materially reduces CFIUS and political scrutiny.
Areas Requiring Attention
National Security (48/100 — High Risk): Company's enterprise AI product suite creates dual-use technology exposure. U.S. counsel review of technology transfer protocols between U.S. and PRC engineering teams is incomplete.
Political & Geopolitical Risk (55/100 — Medium): Sector (AI / enterprise software) is referenced in pending RESTRICT Act provisions. Ongoing monitoring of legislative developments and FARA registration status needs formalization.
Regulatory Scrutiny (62/100 — Medium): Pre-CFIUS filing analysis has been initiated but not completed. Export Control Classification Number (ECCN) determinations for two product lines remain pending.
Financial Readiness (60/100 — Medium): No PCAOB-registered external auditor currently engaged; transition from PRC-registered firm required before any U.S. capital market activity. Currency hedging policy exists but has not been stress-tested for severe RMB/USD volatility scenarios.
Expert Recommendations
1. Engage qualified CFIUS counsel immediately to complete a pre-filing risk assessment and determine whether a voluntary notice is advisable prior to any U.S. investment or partnership announcement.
2. Establish a technology transfer firewall between U.S. and PRC engineering operations, including documented access controls, data segregation policies, and regular audits — critical to reducing the National Security module score.
3. Complete ECCN determinations for all product lines with qualified Export Control Counsel and implement an automated export screening system for U.S.-origin controlled components.
4. Transition to a PCAOB-registered auditor to enable future SEC compliance and remove a significant barrier to U.S. capital market participation.
5. File or confirm FARA registration status for any U.S. lobbying or government-relations activities and establish a formal sanctions monitoring workflow using an automated screening service.
6. Formalize supply chain traceability to Tier 2 suppliers and obtain UFLPA compliance documentation from all Tier 1 suppliers operating in or near Xinjiang to eliminate customs detention risk.
Assessment Methodology

This Trans-Border Risk Assessment and Certification (TBRAC) evaluates organizations across ten comprehensive risk dimensions including regulatory scrutiny, political and geopolitical risk, intellectual property protection, data security and privacy, reputational risk, market access, supply chain integrity, financial and economic resilience, national security exposure, and corporate governance. Each dimension is assessed using ten criteria scored on a 0–10 scale by the company under assessment. Module scores (0–100) are summed and inverted to produce a final risk score where higher values indicate greater risk exposure. The assessment is reviewed by domain experts at Assurance Pacific Assessments LLC with deep experience in cross-border market entry and U.S. regulatory compliance.
